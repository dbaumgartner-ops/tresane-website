Mantras

Simple things, for complicated moments.

Life can feel complicated and overwhelming, and thinking clearly gets harder exactly when it matters most. We have found that a few simple phrases do more in those moments than any framework does.

They are not advice, and they are not a program. They are prompts, small enough to write by hand, that change what you notice. Here are three of ours.

When you are in the middle of it

Never waste a good crisis.

Competing demands, not enough time, still trying to get the thing done. The instinct is to put your head down and push through, and often that is right. This one asks for one more question while you are still in it: what am I learning about myself right now.

Getting it done matters. But the accomplishment is the part that fades, and how you navigated it is the part you carry into whatever comes next.

When you are facing something you are not sure you can do

Always have. Always will.

A new role, a bigger room, the quiet voice asking who you think you are. This one sends you backward before you go forward. Look at what you have already come through, with humble pride rather than arrogance, and notice that you are not the same person who came through it.

That is not confidence. It is evidence. And it matters because nothing ahead is ever fully known, so the only thing you can really bring with you is proof that you have handled unknown before.

When something has gone wrong

Now what?

First comes the honest look. Stop the bleeding, stop whatever will keep getting worse, and hold accountability where it belongs. Skipping that part is not grace, it is avoidance, and everyone in the room can tell the difference.

Then comes the question, and it only works second. Now what. It moves you forward without pretending you were never there, which is the difference between moving on and staying parked in it.

How to use them

Write the one you need on a sticky note and put it where you will see it. That is genuinely the whole method. These are mental models rather than a process, and they work precisely because they are short enough to remember when everything else is loud.
